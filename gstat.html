GSTAT's First Ruling on 'Intermediary' Services — Dow India
The GSTAT's first ruling on what constitutes an "intermediary" under Section 2(13) of the IGST Act...
Insight #90
#GSTAT#Intermediary#PlaceOfSupply
The GSTAT's first ruling on the vexed question of what constitutes an "intermediary" under Section 2(13) of the IGST Act.

The facts: Dow India had paid IGST under RCM treating procurement facilitation services received from Dow Europe GmbH (its Swiss group entity, functioning as the centralised procurement hub for the entire global Dow Group) as an import of services. It subsequently changed its position, arguing that Dow Europe was an "intermediary" — which, under Section 13(8)(b) IGST Act (since omitted by Finance Act 2026 w.e.f. 30.03.2026), would have fixed the place of supply at the supplier's location in Switzerland, outside India, making the transaction non-taxable.

GSTAT applied the three-limb framework from CBIC Circular 159/15/2021 and held:

— while the first condition of minimum three parties (Dow Europe, Dow India, and the third-party foreign suppliers) was satisfied,
— the second condition i.e. two distinct supplies, namely a main supply between two principals and a separate ancillary facilitation supply was not.

The critical reasoning: Dow Europe's procurement hub function is not a subsidiary or facilitative activity rendered for Dow India's benefit; it is Dow Europe's core business, performed for the entire Dow Group globally.

Placing reliance on the Karnataka HC ruling in Columbia Sportswear and the Delhi HC ruling in Blackberry India, GSTAT held that Dow Europe rendered services on its own account as an independent contractor with no authority to bind Dow India, satisfying the exclusionary limb of Section 2(13) — and the transaction remained an import of services taxable in India.

Two important ancillary holdings:

First, GSTAT flagged Revenue's contradictory posture — treating outbound group services by Indian entities as intermediary (to deny export/zero-rating) while simultaneously treating inbound group services from foreign entities as import of services (to levy GST) and observed that both positions cannot simultaneously hold, one consistent view must apply; and

Second, GSTAT correctly disagreed with the First Appellate Authority's "afterthought" reasoning, holding that a refund claim filed under Section 54 CGST Act cannot be dismissed merely because the taxpayer initially paid tax under a mistaken legal understanding — the statutory refund mechanism exists precisely for such situations and must be examined on merits.

GSTAT Appeal Filing — Token System (Order No. 156/2026)
The GSTAT has introduced a new token system to assist with appeal filings under Section 112...
Insight #93
#GSTAT#AppealFiling#TokenSystem
The GSTAT has introduced a new token system to assist with appeal filings under Section 112 of the CGST Act, 2017.

Here are the critical details you need to know:

• Legal Basis: Implemented under Rule 123 of the GSTAT (Procedure) Rules, 2025.
• Meeting the Deadline: If you generate a token on or before July 31, 2026, it is deemed as sufficient compliance for filing within the statutory due date.
• 60-Day Filing Window: You must complete your actual appeal filing within 60 days of the token generation date.
• Delay Fees: By generating a token on time, you avoid the delay fee, provided the filing is completed within the 60-day window.
• Accuracy Requirement: Tokens generated with inaccurate or incomplete details will be treated as void.
• One Token per Appeal: A separate, unique token for every single appeal.
• Eligibility: Open to taxpayers and tax officials holding a GSTIN, Temporary ID, UIN, or TDS Registration.

Order No. 156/2026, issued by Justice Mayank Kumar Jain, dated July 10, 2026.

Cuttack GSTAT Bench: Question of Law Dictates Bench Selection
A recent GSTAT Cuttack State Bench order establishes a critical rule for appeals...
Insight #97
#GSTAT#QOL#DivisionBench
A recent GSTAT Cuttack State Bench order establishes a critical rule for appeals involving both factual disputes and statutory interpretations.

In a dispute over transit penalties and appellate jurisdiction under Section 129(5), the Tribunal explicitly noted that the Revenue's grounds presented a mix of both Questions of Fact (QOF) and Questions of Law (QOL), hence, an appeal involving such mixed question of law and fact, the jurisdiction dictated by the QOL prevails. Accordingly, the appeal was transferred to the Division bench.

Crucially, the Tribunal considered the Revenue's "deemed closure" argument — that paying a penalty under Sec 129(5) permanently concludes proceedings, barring any refund — qualifies to be a QOL.

Takeaway: Taxpayers should be highly cautious and precise in determining if their appeal involves a Question of Law. Properly identifying and framing a statutory interpretation issue provides a strategic opportunity to elevate the matter and ensure it is heard directly by a Division Bench or Principal Bench.

GSTAT: GST Benefit Cannot Be Passed Through Upgrades — Only Through Price Reduction
Hon'ble GSTAT held that benefit of additional ITC under Section 171 cannot be discharged...
Insight #100
#GSTAT#AntiProfiteering#Section171
Hon'ble GSTAT has held that the benefit of additional Input Tax Credit under Section 171 of the CGST Act cannot be discharged by providing free upgrades, structural modifications or additional works in lieu of reducing the price.

A real estate developer claimed that although the sale price of flats was not reduced, the GST benefit had been passed on through additional structural works and fittings whose value exceeded the alleged profiteering amount.

Rejecting the contention, the Tribunal held that Section 171 specifically mandates passing on the benefit by way of commensurate reduction in prices, and this statutory requirement cannot be substituted by any alternate commercial arrangement.

Significantly, the Tribunal also clarified that the Delhi High Court's ruling in Reckitt Benckiser is not confined to the FMCG sector; it interprets Section 171 itself and, therefore, equally governs the real estate sector.

GSTAT Thiruvananthapuram: Section 129 Penalty Not Leviable on Stock Transfer Without E-Way Bill
Hon'ble GSTAT held Section 129 penalty is not leviable where goods are moved...
Insight #108
#GSTAT#Section129#EWayBill
Hon'ble GSTAT has held that Section 129 penalty is not leviable where goods are moved as a stock transfer between two registered premises of the same person without an e-way bill.

While GSTAT clarified that an e-way bill is still required under Rule 138(1)(ii) for movement "for reasons other than supply", it held that such movement is not a "supply" under Section 7 as it involves only one person/entity and no consideration; consequently, there is no tax payable under Section 9.

Since Section 129 penalty is computed with reference to the "tax payable on such goods", the provision cannot be invoked where no tax is payable.

Rejecting Revenue's argument that "tax payable" is merely a measure for quantifying penalty, GSTAT held that the e-way bill lapse in such a case may attract the document-related penalty under Section 122(1)(xiv), but not Section 129.

GSTAT Procedure Rules — Key Amendments Proposed
GSTAT Committee recommended several amendments to GSTAT Procedure Rules, 2025...
Insight #118
#GSTAT#ProcedureRules#Amendments
The GSTAT Committee for Resolution of Trade/Bar Association Representations held its 3rd & 4th meetings under Hon'ble President GSTAT and recommended several amendments to the GSTAT (Procedure) Rules, 2025.

Key proposed changes:

📋 Definitions & Certification: "Certified copy" authentication now permissible by officer of the department, or by the appellant/respondent themselves (Rule 2(f))

📂 Filing & Documents: If the appealed order is uploaded on the common portal, certified copy need not accompany the appeal. Physical document tagging replaced with online filing via e-signature/EVC.

⏱️ Timelines — More Breathing Room: Defect removal extended to 15 working days (was 7); 45 days overall (was 30). Respondent reply: within one month or such extended time as the Bench directs.

📅 Cause List: Daily cause list replaced with a weekly cause list.

📝 IAs: Restoration added as a permissible IA prayer. No IA required for adjournment requests.

🌐 Translation: Bench may relax English translation requirement at its discretion.

💰 Fees: No fee for rectification applications under Section 112(10).

These are Committee recommendations and will take effect only upon formal notification.
