BREAKING: SC Rules Rule 96(10) Cannot Survive in Pending Cases After Its Omission
Hon'ble SC has held that the omission of Rule 96(10) of the CGST Rules with effect from 8 October 2024 applies even to pending refund proceedings...
Insight #157
#SupremeCourt#Rule96#IGSTRefund
Hon'ble SC has held that the omission of Rule 96(10) of the CGST Rules with effect from 8 October 2024 applies even to pending refund proceedings.

The Court relied on the Constitution Bench ruling in Kolhapur Canesugar Works and held that when a rule is omitted, pending proceedings under that rule can continue only if there is a saving clause or other legal mechanism preserving them.

Since Rule 96(10) was omitted without any saving clause, the restriction cannot continue to apply merely because the refund proceedings were pending when the rule was omitted.

Interestingly, the Court also noted that the GST Council's recommendation for prospective omission was only advisory and could not override the legal effect of the omission.

Impact: Pending IGST refund disputes involving Rule 96(10) will now have to be considered without applying the omitted restriction.

The Supreme Court has also directed High Courts to take up pending matters on this issue expeditiously to bring the litigation to a close.

GST Amnesty: Madras HC Says Self-Assessed Tax Cannot Shut the Door on Amnesty
The Madras High Court has held that self-assessed tax cannot, by itself, take a taxpayer out of the GST Amnesty Scheme...
Insight #158
#GSTAmnesty#Section73#Rule164
The Madras High Court has held that self-assessed tax cannot, by itself, take a taxpayer out of the GST Amnesty Scheme, where proceedings have been initiated under Section 73. The Court also held that CBIC Circular 238 cannot restrict the benefit available under the Act.

Importantly, the Court held that the timelines under Rule 164 are binding on the Department. If the officer fails to pass an order within the prescribed time, the waiver application can be deemed to have been approved, subject to the taxpayer meeting the substantive conditions of the Amnesty Scheme.

The Court also set aside "void orders" cancelling earlier waiver approvals where the conditions for making the approval void were not met.

However, taxpayers must also comply with their own timelines — belated applications or belated payment will not qualify for the Amnesty benefit.

GST Council Can Recommend, Not Ratify: Madras HC Draws a Crucial Constitutional Line
In a significant GST ruling, the Madras High Court held that the Government cannot, through delegated legislation, travel beyond recommendations...
Insight #159
#GSTCouncil#Article279A#DelegatedLegislation
In a significant GST ruling, the Madras High Court held that the Government cannot, through delegated legislation, travel beyond the recommendations of the GST Council.

The dispute arose because the GST Council had recommended taxation of goods bearing a brand name on which an "actionable claim" was available, whereas the subsequent GST Notifications expanded the levy to cover brand names carrying an "enforceable right in a court of law".

Striking down the expanded portion as ultra vires, the Court held that while Mohit Minerals recognised that GST Council recommendations are not binding on Parliament or State Legislatures when enacting primary legislation, a different standard applies to subordinate legislation issued under Sections 9 and 11 of the CGST Act, which must remain within the contours of the Council's recommendation.

Equally significant was the Court's rejection of the Revenue's plea that the GST Council's later ratification cured the defect. According to the Court, Article 279A empowers the Council only to make recommendations and does not confer any power of ratification; consequently, an invalid exercise of delegated legislative power cannot be retrospectively validated through a subsequent Council approval.

SC: Assessment Order Upload on GST Portal = Valid Service | Ignorance No Excuse
Hon'ble Supreme Court dismissed an SLP filed against a Section 74 assessment order...
Insight #161
#GSTPortal#ElectronicService#Section74
Hon'ble Supreme Court dismissed an SLP filed against a Section 74 assessment order, making two critical observations.

First, upload of an assessment order on the electronic portal constitutes valid communication — no convincing reason exists to overlook electronic service, and ignorance of a portal-uploaded order will not be accepted.

Second, and independently, the petitioner's own admission of having become aware of proceedings in 2024 yet approaching the High Court by writ only in 2025 — by which time Section 107 appeal was time-barred — was fatal.

The Court firmly held that a writ petition cannot serve as a surrogate for an appellate remedy the petitioner chose not to avail.

AIFTP Seeks One-Time Amnesty for Delayed First Appeals
The All India Federation of Tax Practitioners (AIFTP) has represented to the Finance Minister...
Insight #162
#AIFTP#Amnesty#Section107
The All India Federation of Tax Practitioners (AIFTP) has represented to the Finance Minister, GST Council, CBIC and GSTAT seeking a one-time amnesty scheme to condone delays in filing first appeals under Section 107 of the CGST Act.

AIFTP proposes that appeals dismissed by First Appellate Authorities solely on limitation be restored and decided on merits, including through a suo motu restoration mechanism.

It argues that genuine delays have often arisen from portal issues, improper service, administrative difficulties, evolving GST law and other bona fide circumstances, resulting in substantive disputes remaining unexamined.

AIFTP further submits that no additional 10% pre-deposit should be required for restoration where the statutory pre-deposit was already made.

Supreme Court Affirms: Wind Turbine Generators Are Movable Goods, Not Immovable Under GST
The legal position that Wind Turbine Generators (WTGs) are movable goods under GST has gained major reinforcement...
Insight #164
#WindEnergy#MovableGoods#WorksContract
The legal position that Wind Turbine Generators (WTGs) are movable goods under GST has gained major reinforcement, as the Hon'ble Supreme Court recently dismissed the Revenue's Special Leave Petition (SLP) against the landmark Andhra Pradesh High Court ruling.

Hon'ble AP High Court struck down the department's attempt to classify WTG turnkey contracts as an 18% "works contract" for immovable property.

The Court held that the massive earth-embedded civil foundation exists purely for the operational stability of the machinery against high wind loads.

This ruling has significant implications for the renewable energy sector, confirming that WTG installations should not be treated as immovable property for GST purposes.

Adjust IGST Against CGST & SGST Liability in Case of Payment Under Wrong Heads — Karnataka HC
Karnataka HC clarified that IGST paid mistakenly on transactions subsequently determined to be intra-state...
Insight #166
#IGST#WrongHead#Section77
In a significant ruling regarding GST compliance, Hon'ble High Court has clarified that IGST paid mistakenly on transactions that are subsequently determined to be intra-state supplies must be adjusted against CGST/SGST liability.

Relying on Section 77(2) of the CGST/KGST Act and Rule 92 of the GST Rules, the Court emphasized that authorities cannot demand fresh payments, interest, or penalties when the taxpayer has already remitted tax under the wrong head, provided there is no revenue loss to the government.

Consequently, the Court quashed the existing adjudication order and remanded the matter to the original authority, directing them to properly consider the adjustment of the taxes paid rather than enforcing a fresh demand.

E-Commerce Contracts by Karnataka HO — Can Another State Demand GST? Allahabad HC Stays Recovery
Allahabad HC stayed recovery in a GST dispute involving an e-commerce operator...
Insight #167
#ECommerce#PlaceOfSupply#Jurisdiction
Allahabad HC has stayed recovery in a GST dispute involving an e-commerce operator, where the Revenue sought to treat marketplace and allied services as intra-State supplies in Uttar Pradesh.

The assessee contended that the disputed transactions were undertaken through its Karnataka head office/registration: agreements with registered sellers were entered into through Karnataka, invoices were issued from the Karnataka GSTIN and IGST was duly discharged in Karnataka. It further submitted that, except for call-centre operations and activities relating to TCS, it did not undertake substantial activities in UP in relation to the disputed supplies.

The assessee argued that the location of the supplier was Karnataka, while the applicable place-of-supply provisions pointed to the location of the respective registered sellers, making the supplies inter-State supplies. It therefore challenged the jurisdiction of the UP authorities to levy UPGST on these transactions.

Significantly, the assessee contended that the SCNs did not disclose the jurisdictional fact on the basis of which transactions already subjected to IGST could be re-characterised as intra-State supplies.

The key issue: Where an e-commerce operator enters into contracts through its Karnataka head office, invoices from its Karnataka GST registration and pays IGST, can the same transactions be treated as intra-State supplies in UP?

The HC has presently stayed recovery. The underlying jurisdiction and place-of-supply issues remain to be decided.

Misclassified Tax Cannot Become a Lawful Levy, Hence Cannot Be Retained by Invoking Limitation — Madras HC
Hon'ble Madras High Court has upheld refund of service tax paid on supply of floating rigs...
Insight #168
#Misclassification#Limitation#Article265
Hon'ble Madras High Court has upheld refund of service tax paid on supply of floating rigs under the classification of "Mining Services" for the period prior to 16.05.2008.

The Court noted that the Supreme Court had held such services to be classifiable under "Supply of Tangible Goods Service", which became taxable only from 16.05.2008.

Importantly, on the issue of limitation, the Court held that "misclassification and payment of service tax erroneously can never carry the character of lawful levy" and that tax collected under such mistake of law cannot be retained by the Government by citing limitation or technical grounds, in view of Article 265 of the Constitution.

The Court therefore held Section 11B limitation inapplicable to the refund claim in the facts of the case.

The Court also rejected Revenue's reliance on Mafatlal Industries, holding that its factual matrix was different. Here, the refund had already been allowed by the First Appellate Authority and affirmed by CESTAT, with the classification issue having been settled.
