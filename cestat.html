Landmark CESTAT Ruling: Transfer Pricing Does Not Determine Customs Valuation
The importer determined the value of goods imported from its overseas group entities...
Insight #120
#CESTAT#TransferPricing#CustomsValuation
Brief Background: The importer determined the value of goods imported from its overseas group entities under a global ROVAC (Return on Value Added Cost) transfer pricing policy, designed to ensure that the Indian distributor earned a predetermined return. Customs disputed the declared import values, leading the Tribunal to undertake an extensive examination of the interplay between transfer pricing and customs valuation.

Key Tribunal Observations:

📌 Transfer Pricing (TP) Rules ≠ Customs Valuation Rules
The Tribunal held that OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and income-tax principles serve a different purpose from Customs valuation. While transfer pricing examines profitability of associated enterprises over a representative period, Customs valuation is transaction-specific and governed exclusively by Section 14 of the Customs Act and the Customs Valuation Rules. Accordingly, TP documentation cannot, by itself, establish acceptability of the declared customs value.

📌 Buyer's Arm's Length Margin Alone Cannot Validate Import Price
A pricing methodology designed to ensure the Indian distributor earns a predetermined return does not automatically establish that the declared import price satisfies the Customs Valuation Rules.

📌 Higher Income-Tax Payment Does Not Cure Customs Valuation
Higher profits and consequent higher income-tax payment in India do not establish that the declared import value satisfies the Customs Valuation Rules.

📌 Deductive Valuation Cannot Result in Double Additions
Having adopted the deductive value method, Customs cannot again add international freight and insurance where those elements are already embedded in the resale price.

📌 Discounts Must Independently Satisfy Customs Valuation Rules
Each category of discount — special negotiated discounts, price protection, end-of-life discounts, cooperative marketing discounts and influencer payments — was examined separately. Admissibility depends on the Customs Valuation Rules and evidence, rather than treatment under the transfer pricing policy.

#CESTAT#ExportBenefits#ReImport
The CESTAT Ahmedabad, in the case of INOX India Pvt. Ltd., has ruled that an exporter is not required to reverse export benefits (such as duty drawback) when re-importing defective goods, provided that a replacement consignment has already been exported to the customer.

CESTAT Chandigarh: CENVAT Credit Can't Be Denied Even if Cost Is Reimbursed
In Yatra Online, airlines reimbursed payment gateway charges to assessee...
Insight #122
#CESTAT#CENVATCredit#Reimbursement
In Yatra Online, airlines reimbursed payment gateway charges to the assessee. The department argued credit was not admissible since the assessee didn't "bear the cost" / there was "no value addition."

CESTAT's finding: That logic has no legal backing. CENVAT Credit Rules don't restrict eligibility merely because expenses are reimbursed. The real test is whether the service is an "input service" for providing output services.

Strong defence for businesses where costs are contractually reimbursed but still integral to taxable output services.

'Unjust Enrichment' Applies to Exports — CESTAT Hyderabad
CESTAT Hyderabad in Sesa Sterlite reaffirms unjust enrichment applies to export duty refunds...
Insight #123
#CESTAT#UnjustEnrichment#ExportDuty
Hon'ble CESTAT recently in the case of M/s Sesa Sterlite Ltd reaffirms that the doctrine of Unjust Enrichment (UE) is applicable to export duty refunds, challenging the common industry belief that exports are naturally exempt.

The Tribunal held that if an exporter realizes proceeds higher than the declared FOB value in their Shipping Bill, a legal presumption arises that the duty burden was passed on to the overseas buyer.

To secure a refund, exporters must now provide granular financial evidence and clear Bank Realization Certificates (BRC) to prove they haven't been "doubly enriched" by both the buyer and a government refund.

Insight #125
#CESTAT#GSTAT#Jurisdiction
The Ahmedabad CESTAT has officially referred a critical jurisdictional deadlock to a Larger Bench to decide who holds the final authority over IGST on imports.

In a high-stakes case involving lithium-ion battery parts, the Tribunal paused the merits of the dispute to address a fundamental question:

Can a Customs-created body legally interpret GST rate schedules?

With past rulings clashing and the government often exempting IGST via Customs notifications, the Bench identified a "jurisdictional conflict" that must be settled.

This decision is relevant for importers, as it will finally define whether border tax battles belong in the Customs Tribunal or the newly formed GST Appellate Tribunal.

Crucially, this reference creates a ripple effect: until the Larger Bench finalizes the jurisdiction, all existing IGST matters before CESTAT or appeals before GSTAT are likely to be held up or stayed.

Unaccepted Price Hike Can't Be Taxed — CESTAT Chennai
CESTAT Chennai held differential duty paid on supplementary invoice was not legally payable...
Insight #127
#CESTAT#Valuation#Section4
CESTAT Chennai held that differential duty paid on a supplementary invoice raised after the assessee unilaterally increased the price from ₹260 to ₹390 per unit due to cost escalation was not legally payable since the buyer (Visteon) never accepted the revised price, never paid the differential amount, and never took credit.

The Tribunal held that under Section 4, assessable value is the "price actually paid or payable" at the time of removal, and a unilateral, unaccepted supplementary invoice cannot create a new transaction value.

With the enhanced price never crystallizing into a legally enforceable obligation, the duty paid was only notional and refundable. The bar of unjust enrichment was also rejected based on clear evidence that the incidence was not passed on.

NHAI Granting "Toll Plaza Collection Rights" Is a 'Service' Not 'Sovereign' — CESTAT Hyderabad
CESTAT Hyderabad upheld Service Tax demands on lump-sum consideration NHAI receives...
Insight #128
#CESTAT#NHAI#TollRights
The CESTAT Hyderabad recently upheld Service Tax demands on the lump-sum consideration NHAI receives from contractors in exchange for transferring "user fee collection rights."

Dismissing the defense that toll collection is an inalienable sovereign function immune under the negative list, the Tribunal ruled that assigning these rights via competitive bidding for a fixed amount constitutes a commercial business activity.

Substantively, the Tribunal held this transfer qualifies as a taxable "franchise service" pre-July 2012 and a general taxable service thereafter.

Revenue Sharing Is Not Consideration for a Service — CESTAT Kolkata
Kolkata CESTAT set aside service tax demand on Dhamra Port arising from rail connectivity...
Insight #130
#CESTAT#RevenueSharing#ServiceTax
The Kolkata CESTAT has set aside a service tax demand on Dhamra Port arising from its rail connectivity project with Indian Railways.

The Department had argued that by constructing and maintaining a dedicated railway line, the port was providing "infrastructural support" to Railways and that the 95% share of freight revenue received by it constituted consideration for a taxable Business Support Service.

Rejecting this view, the Tribunal held that the arrangement was not one of service provider and service recipient. Instead, the port had invested its own funds to create the rail infrastructure, while Railways operated the trains and collected freight, with revenues being shared under the Ministry of Railways' Participative Investment Model.

The Tribunal observed that the freight share was essentially a mechanism to return the port's capital investment with a reasonable profit and, in the absence of any identifiable quid pro quo, a revenue-sharing arrangement could not be treated as consideration for a taxable service.

CESTAT Mumbai: Pre-Deposit Interest Applicability — Date of Deposit Controls
CESTAT Mumbai held statutory regime governing interest on refunded pre-deposits...
Insight #131
#CESTAT#PreDeposit#InterestRegime
In a strict textual interpretation, Hon'ble CESTAT Mumbai has held that the statutory regime governing interest on refunded pre-deposits is determined strictly by the date the deposit was made, not the date of the subsequent appellate order or refund claim.

Relying on the explicit savings proviso in the amended Section 35FF of the Central Excise Act (effective 06.08.2014), the Tribunal affirmed that deposits made prior to the amendment remain bound by the erstwhile unamended provision, which granted a three-month interest-free grace period to the Department.

Consequently, since the principal was refunded within three months of the favorable appellate order, the taxpayer was ordered to repay the day-one interest erroneously granted by the original adjudicating authority.

Parallel Battle Brewing Under GST: A remarkably similar transitional controversy is currently playing out regarding the recent mandatory 10% pre-deposit condition for penalty-only appeals — whether the cut-off date attaches to the date of the underlying SCN or the date of filing appeal post-amendment.

"Natural Causes" Includes Inevitable Manufacturing Process Losses — CESTAT Kolkata
CESTAT Kolkata held natural causes and unavoidable accident under Rule 21...
Insight #132
#CESTAT#Rule21#ProcessLoss
Hon'ble CESTAT Kolkata has held that the expressions "natural causes" and "unavoidable accident" appearing in Rule 21 of the Central Excise Rules, 2002 must be given a reasonable and liberal interpretation to encompass inevitable and irrecoverable handling and process losses that are inherent to the manufacturing activity and cannot be confined to catastrophic events alone.

In the present case, the Appellant-manufacturer of pig iron (Odisha Mining Corporation) suffered a shortage of 0.55% (against total production over 11 years) owing to generation of pig iron chips, dust, and dross — an inevitable consequence of the manufacturing process.

Setting aside the Commissioner's order rejecting remission, the Tribunal observed that since the loss was undisputedly inevitable and irrecoverable, and well within the Board's prescribed condonable limit of 2% (which constitutes binding instructions on Revenue), the remission under Rule 21 ought to have been granted.

CESTAT: SEZ Exemption Cannot Be Denied Merely for Non-Furnishing of Form A-2
Hyderabad CESTAT reaffirmed statutory tax exemption available to SEZ units...
Insight #133
#CESTAT#SEZ#FormA2
Hon'ble Hyderabad CESTAT has reaffirmed that the statutory tax exemption available to SEZ units cannot be denied merely for non-furnishing of Form A-2.

The Tribunal held that Section 26 of the SEZ Act confers a substantive exemption, and the conditions governing such exemption must flow from the SEZ Act and SEZ Rules, not from procedural requirements prescribed through notifications issued under the Finance Act.

Invoking the overriding effect of Section 51 of the SEZ Act, the Tribunal observed that where it is undisputed that the services were provided to an eligible SEZ unit for authorised operations, exemption cannot be denied solely because Form A-2 was not furnished.

As there was no finding that the services were ineligible or not used for authorised operations, the demand of service tax, interest and penalty was set aside, with the matter remanded only for limited re-quantification of the late fee on delayed ST-3 returns.

No Limitation Period Where Two Parties Make Service Tax Payments on Same Service — CESTAT
CESTAT held where service tax is discharged by provider and recipient also pays...
Insight #135
#CESTAT#DoubleTaxPayment#Limitation
Hon'ble CESTAT has categorically held that where service tax has already been discharged by the service provider, and the recipient also pays service tax on the same activity under a mistaken belief that RCM applies, such erroneous payment is NOT DUTY at all — it is a mere deposit made without authority of law.

Since the Revenue never had the jurisdiction to collect or retain an amount that was never legally payable in the first place (the tax liability having already stood discharged by the provider), the one-year limitation prescribed under Section 11B of the Central Excise Act simply cannot be invoked to deny the refund.

The Tribunal reinforced that Articles 265 and 300A of the Constitution protect the assessee — no tax can be retained without authority of law, and the procedural limitation meant for lawfully collected duty cannot become a tool for the State to unjustly enrich itself from amounts it was never entitled to collect.

EOU Benefit Cannot Be Denied Merely Because Duty-Free Machinery Was Used Outside the Factory
CESTAT held duty exemption on capital goods cannot be denied merely because machinery was used at approved quarry...
Insight #136
#CESTAT#EOU#CapitalGoods
In an important ruling on the EOU scheme, CESTAT has held that duty exemption on capital goods cannot be denied merely because the machinery was used at an approved quarry instead of the main manufacturing unit.

The Tribunal observed that the expression "for the purpose of manufacture" is much wider than machinery used in the actual manufacturing process.

Since quarrying was undertaken at an approved EOU location to extract granite blocks — the basic raw material for the export product — it formed an integral part of the manufacturing chain.

The Tribunal also emphasised that EOU exemption notifications must be interpreted purposively to promote exports and that benefits cannot be denied where all movements and operations were carried out with the approval and knowledge of the authorities.

MOOWR Scheme — No Interest Leviable on Ex-Bonded Capital Goods if 'Intended for Use'
CESTAT examined whether interest is payable when capital goods imported under MOOWR are cleared...
Insight #137
#CESTAT#MOOWR#IntendedUse
Hon'ble CESTAT examined whether interest is payable when capital goods imported under the MOOWR scheme are cleared for home consumption (ex-bonded) without actually being put to use in the warehouse.

Setting aside the revenue's demand, the Tribunal held that the law only requires the goods to be "intended for use" at the time of warehousing, rather than mandating actual physical usage.

Relying on Supreme Court precedents, the Tribunal clarified that if subsequent operational or commercial changes prevent the actual usage of the machinery, the initial bona fide intent is not negated.

Therefore, clearing such capital goods upon payment of the deferred customs duty does not attract any interest.

Forfeited Insurance Premium Not Taxable as a Separate Service — CESTAT Mumbai
Mumbai CESTAT held forfeiture of life insurance premium on account of policy lapse...
Insight #138
#CESTAT#Insurance#ForfeitedPremium
Hon'ble Mumbai CESTAT has held that forfeiture of life insurance premium on account of policy lapse due to non-payment of premium or repudiation for mis-declaration cannot be subjected to Service Tax as a separate service.

The Tribunal observed that the insurance contract is fundamentally for providing life insurance and not for tolerating breach by the policyholder. There is neither an independent contractual obligation to tolerate such default nor any separate flow of consideration for the alleged act of tolerance.

Relying on CBIC Circular No. 214/1/2023 and settled judicial precedents, the Tribunal further held that treating the forfeited premium as consideration for a separate service would effectively result in double taxation, as Service Tax had already been discharged on the insurance premium.

Import Duty Payable on Actual Reduced Price Despite Higher Price at Time of Import — CESTAT Mumbai
Mumbai CESTAT upheld customs valuation based on actual reduced purchase price...
Insight #139
#CESTAT#CustomsValuation#TransactionValue
In a majority order, the Mumbai CESTAT has upheld customs valuation based on the actual reduced purchase price, even though the goods had originally arrived in India under a much higher contracted price.

The Tribunal noted that the original importer neither honoured the letter of credit, nor filed the Bill of Entry, nor took delivery of the goods. As the consignment remained uncleared, the overseas supplier entered into a fresh contract with another buyer at a significantly reduced price to mitigate mounting detention and demurrage charges.

The majority held that the earlier transaction never culminated in import and, therefore, could not determine the assessable value. Applying Section 14, it held that the transaction which actually resulted in import and clearance of the goods alone was relevant.

Since the declared price represented the actual consideration and none of the statutory grounds for rejecting the transaction value were established, the reduced renegotiated price was accepted for customs valuation and the Revenue's appeal was dismissed.

No Extra Condition Can Be Read Into the Description of an Exemption Notification — CESTAT Chennai
Chennai CESTAT allowed appeals concerning concessional rate under Notification 2/2011-CE...
Insight #140
#CESTAT#ExemptionNotification#EndUse
The Chennai CESTAT has allowed appeals concerning the concessional rate under Sl. No. 17 of Notification No. 2/2011-CE, covering "Anaesthetics."

An important aspect of the ruling is the distinction between the description of the goods and an end-use requirement.

The assessee's (Inox Air Products) invoices described the product as "Nitrous Oxide I.P." The Tribunal examined the character of the goods and held that pharmacopoeial-grade Nitrous Oxide manufactured as a medical anaesthetic answers the notified description "Anaesthetics."

The Tribunal held that the notification neither prescribed an end-use condition nor required end-use certification or verification. Therefore, Revenue could not deny the benefit merely because the product had multiple uses or was supplied through traders.

An end-use requirement could not be introduced by implication when it was not contained in the notification.

SC: Composite Supplies Cannot Be Artificially Split to Tax a Levy Introduced Only Subsequently
Supreme Court upheld CESTAT ruling that turnkey contracts for ATMs executed prior to June 2007...
Insight #144
#SupremeCourt#CESTAT#CompositeSupply
Hon'ble Supreme Court has dismissed the Revenue's appeal and upheld the CESTAT ruling that turnkey contracts for supply, installation and commissioning of ATMs executed prior to 1 June 2007 cannot be vivisected to levy service tax under "Commissioning or Installation Service."

The Tribunal held that the contracts were indivisible works contracts executed for a lump-sum consideration, with installation and commissioning being merely incidental to the supply of ATMs.

Since neither the contracts nor the invoices segregated the value of goods and services, the entire transaction retained its composite character and could not be subjected to service tax before works contracts were specifically brought within the tax net from 1 June 2007.

The decision may have a much wider significance. It reinforces the principle that a transaction cannot be fastened to a levy that was introduced only at a later point in time.

This could be relevant in the ongoing controversy surrounding taxation of corporate guarantees, etc.

Supreme Court upheld taxability of referral fees and advertisement reimbursements...
Insight #145
#SupremeCourt#CESTAT#OEMReferralFees
Hon'ble Supreme Court has upheld the taxability of referral fees and advertisement reimbursements received by auto OEMs from banks and insurers.

The Apex Court confirmed that the contractual arrangements go well beyond incidental facilitation, as the OEM was contractually required to identify prospective buyers, share customer databases, provide physical showroom infrastructure, and use the bank's branding in advertisements.

While the tax stands, the Court completely dropped the penalties originally sustained by the tribunal.

SC Landmark: P2P Model Overturned — Service Tax Imposed on OMC Trade Margins
Supreme Court delivered landmark verdict in BPCL/HPCL matter overturning precedents...
Insight #146
#SupremeCourt#CESTAT#P2PModel
Hon'ble Supreme Court delivered a landmark verdict in the BPCL/HPCL matter, overturning long-standing Tribunal precedents and allowing the Revenue's appeal, effectively upholding the levy of service tax on trade margins in CNG distribution.

The Court applied a substance-over-form approach, reasoning that:

(a) the supplier retained complete control over pricing, equipment, and operations;
(b) title/ownership of CNG never transferred to the OMCs at any point;
(c) risk of supply deficiency remained entirely with the supplier;
(d) the agreement expressly provided for "commission in consideration of services provided" — payable per kg actually sold, a hallmark of agency remuneration;
(e) upon termination, unsold CNG was to be returned to the supplier;
(f) the OMCs' role was limited to providing site, infrastructure, manpower, and marketing facilitation.

The Court rejected the argument that VAT payment conclusively established a sale, holding that form of tax compliance cannot override the substantive legal relationship.
