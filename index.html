Hon'ble Calcutta HC Upholds 9% Interest & Rejects 6% Cap on Mistake-of-Law Refunds!
When tax is deposited under a mistake of law, it does not bear the character of duty, taking it outside strict Section 11B limitations...
Insight #199 September 9, 2026
#CalcuttaHC#Refunds#Interest#IndirectTax
When tax is deposited under a mistake of law, it does not bear the character of duty, taking it outside strict Section 11B limitations and rendering the statutory 6% notification cap inapplicable - fully justifying a robust 9% compensatory interest rate.

Reaffirming this, Hon'ble Calcutta High Court further ruled that Section 11BB interest runs automatically from the expiry of three months from the original refund application date, completely blocking revenue's attempts to reckon delay from subsequent document submissions or appellate orders.

Hon’ble Supreme Court has reserved its order on a plea seeking the statutory regulation of pharmaceutical marketing practices...
Insight #198 September 9, 2026
#SupremeCourt#Pharma#UCPMP#Healthcare
Hon’ble Supreme Court has reserved its order on a plea seeking the statutory regulation of pharmaceutical marketing practices, following submissions by Solicitor General Tushar Mehta that the Union government will constitute a three-member committee to examine the framework within two months.

While the voluntary Uniform Code for Pharmaceutical Marketing Practices (UCPMP) 2024 remains in effect interimly, petitioners underscored a legislative gap wherein doctors face disciplinary penalties under medical council regulations for accepting inducements, yet companies offering them lack corresponding direct statutory penalties invoking the Supreme Court's 2022 income tax judgment in Apex Laboratories Pvt. Ltd. v. Deputy Commissioner of Income Tax, which held that such freebies create a "publicly injurious cycle" and that prohibitions against doctors accepting gifts implicitly extend to the givers.

CENVAT Credit Inadmissible on Capital Goods Imported by Separate Legal Entity Even If the Power Is Used for Captive Manufacturing of Taxable Products
Hon'ble CESTAT Chennai held that tax credit is a strict statutory concession that cannot be availed by one incorporated entity on capital goods imported...
Insight #197 September 8, 2026
#CESTAT#CENVATCredit#CapitalGoods#SeparateLegalEntity
Hon'ble CESTAT Chennai held that tax credit is a strict statutory concession that cannot be availed by one incorporated entity on capital goods imported and duty-paid by a separate independent company, even if the power generated is used exclusively for the captive manufacturing of excisable products.

The bench emphasized that majority shareholding, captive power arrangements, and economic integration cannot override the fundamental corporate law doctrine of separate legal personality or bypass the strict procedural conditions prescribed under the CENVAT Credit Rules, 2004.

WB AAR: Pure‑Agent exclusion allowed even if Rule 33 not met; No GST on electricity reimbursements at actuals
Hon’ble West Bengal AAR has held that recovery of electricity charges at actual cost, without any mark‑up, can qualify as pure agent reimbursement...
Insight #196 September 8, 2026
#WBAAR#PureAgent#GST#ElectricityReimbursement
Hon’ble West Bengal AAR has held that recovery of electricity charges at actual cost, without any mark‑up, can qualify as pure agent reimbursement and remain outside GST.

Two significant observations stand out:

• Rule 33 vs Circular: Even after finding that the applicant fails the pure‑agent test under Rule 33 of the CGST Rules, the AAR held that the deeming fiction in the second sentence of Para 3.3 still applies – where electricity is recovered from occupants at the same amount charged by the DISCOM and without any profit element, the supplier is deemed to act as a pure agent, and GST does not apply.

• Scope widened: The AAR noted that Para 3.3 of Circular No. 206/18/2023‑GST begins with the word “however”, creating an exception to the general principle that electricity supplied along with maintenance/renting forms part of a taxable composite supply. Importantly, the expression “etc.” after Real Estate Owners, RWAs and Developers widens the scope of the Circular to other entities as well, so long as the nature of supply is similar.

Impact: The Circular’s deeming provision operates independently of strict Rule 33 conditions. This reasoning may support actual‑cost electricity recovery by facility managers, maintenance companies and similar arrangements, beyond traditional real estate setups.

Subsequent sale or remanufacturing of imported goods determines customs value
Recently the Hon’ble US CBP has addressed valuation of used, damaged or defective transaxle “cores” returned to the US under a core-exchange programme...
Insight #195 September 5, 2026
#Customs#Valuation#USCBP
Recently the Hon’ble US CBP has addressed valuation of used, damaged or defective transaxle “cores” returned to the US under a core-exchange programme.

• Toyota Canada returned used cores to its US remanufacturer. The cores were not sold to the US at the time of import; the payment received was only a refund of the earlier $1,000 core deposit.

• CBP therefore held that the transaction value method was not available.

• Instead, CBP permitted use of the fallback method, with the eventual treatment of the imported core becoming relevant:

    – If the core is subsequently sold as-is → value can be based on the sale price of comparable cores.

    – If the core is remanufactured → value can be based on the subsequent sale price of the remanufactured product, less the average cost of repair/remanufacturing.

• Since the importer may not know at the time of import which route the core will take, CBP recommended Reconciliation to determine the final value subsequently.

📌 The interesting takeaway: Even though there is no sale at the time of import, subsequent commercial transactions can provide the basis for determining customs value under the fallback method.
